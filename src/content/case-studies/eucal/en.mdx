## Summary

EUCAL calibrates, services and sells laboratory pipettes, with accredited calibration under ISO/IEC 17025. It began in 1998 repairing Eppendorf pipettes and is now an authorised Eppendorf service centre. Its customers are lab staff and purchasers, mostly in Czechia and Slovakia. The site presents its services in Czech, English and German and turns visits into enquiries, repair requests and orders that arrive in EUCAL's mailbox.

## What I owned

I built the site in Next.js in autumn 2024 and moved its content into Sanity, so EUCAL staff edit text, images and attachments in every language without a developer. In 2025 I added the English and German versions and a sales page. In October 2026 I reworked the forms, accessibility and tests. The design is mine too, apart from the logo, and nobody else worked on the code. The repository runs under the client's own GitHub account.

<Decision
  n={2}

  chose="Keep ordering light: the order creator fills in an order PDF in the browser, and the signed order reaches EUCAL by email"
  insteadOf="An e-shop with a database, customer accounts and order history"
  because="The lab already runs everything from its mailbox. The order creator feels like an e-shop, but it is only a helper that makes ordering easier for customers and smooths the process for EUCAL. A database would add hosting, a back office and personal data to look after, for little gain."

## Outcome

The forms used to post straight to a third-party form service, which handled the order attachment badly and could not tell the page whether anything was sent. They now go through the site's own server, which checks every field and file and shows a clear error instead of a false success.

## What I would <Re>re</Re>think

Today I would make ordering one step: the customer submits the form, the server builds the PDF and the customer gets a confirmation email. I would also offer saved details for returning labs. Back then I lacked the backend experience; with AI the extra work is small. I would still keep email as the place where orders arrive.
