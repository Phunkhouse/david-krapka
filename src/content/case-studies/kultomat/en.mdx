## Summary

Kultomat helps people in Prague quickly find a worthwhile film they can actually go and see, especially an older or culturally significant one. Open it on a phone and it lists classics really screening in the next seven days, grouped by film, with a short reason each one is worth seeing and a link to the cinema's own page.

It went live on 26 October 2026 with seven cinemas. On 7 November it covered 17 cinemas from 13 sources. Anyone can browse without an account; a watchlist with email alerts is open to invited friends and family.

## What I owned

All of it: the question, the product and data rules, the design, the code and running it. It started as a research project on 24 October 2026 and the first public release was live two days later.

I wrote the specification, the milestones and a decision record for every significant choice. I also have an ongoing role inside the product: each week I review the films the matcher was unsure about, and I decide which classics the site recommends editorially.

## Problem and constraints

Prague's cinemas each publish their programme on their own website. Finding an older film worth seeing this week means opening many of them and knowing which titles matter. It was my own problem first: I want to see classics on a big screen with friends.

The constraints shaped everything:

- **One person, no budget.** Free tiers only: the database, the web hosting, 100 emails a day and a non-commercial film API.
- **No feeds.** No cinema offers a documented feed or a reuse licence, so every source is a public web page that can change without notice. Each source gets its own access decision, and Kultomat respects robots.txt, never copies synopses or posters and always links back.
- **Honesty.** No claims about free seats, no simulated data, and a broken source must never look like a wave of cancellations.

## The build

A worker on the Pi reads each cinema's public programme about four times a day. It keeps the raw response and records where and when every fact was seen. It matches programme entries to films through TMDB and Wikidata, merges copies of the same screening across sources, and turns the differences between runs into events: a new showtime, a move, a cancellation or a reinstatement. A run that fails changes nothing, so a broken source never cancels anything.

Automatic matching makes mistakes, so they have a place to go. A private, phone-friendly admin page holds a weekly queue where I confirm or correct matches and pick classics, with an audit log and undo, and without a deploy.

The web app is Next.js, Czech first with a complete English version. Public pages are cached and refreshed after each ingest, so visitors rarely wake the database, and they read through a database role that cannot see personal data even through a bug.

## AI and collaboration

I worked with AI coding agents. I wrote the specification, the milestones and the decision records in the repository, and agents implemented them one pull request at a time, about 250 by 8 November 2026. Agents also prepared the dated source research and the audits, which I read and decided on.

Kultomat itself calls no AI model. The short texts about each film are mine or adapted from Wikipedia with attribution, and a text drafted with an AI tool outside Kultomat says so on the page.

## What I would <Re>re</Re>think

The classic rule changed on first contact with real data: old films get far fewer votes, so they needed a lower bar, and some classics need a person to vouch for them. I would plan for an editorial layer from the start instead of hoping the numbers are enough.

Some things I chose not to build yet. Push notifications wait until email turns out too slow, public sign-up waits for the privacy work it needs, and AI-drafted film texts wait for TMDB to confirm the use is allowed.

Hosting is the one I had to redo. Vercel's free plan has no clearly documented data processing agreement, which matters once the public is invited. Before the public launch I moved the web app to Netlify's free plan, whose agreement covers it, and kept Vercel as a warm standby: switching back is a DNS change, plus the Pro plan if the agreement is needed.
