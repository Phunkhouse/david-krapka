## Observed gaps

### LEG-003: Email transfer information omits the Swiss receiving provider

- Severity: **Medium**; confidence: **High**; status: **Confirmed**; applicability: **Applies**; verification: **Gap**.
- Previous requirement: LEG-003, 4 November planning report; transparency is implemented in part, but actual email routing was unresolved then.
- Location: `src/content/privacy-notice.ts:69`, `:81` and Czech equivalents `:163`, `:175`; built `/privacy/` and `/cs/privacy/`.
- Evidence: both notices describe forwarded email, list email-provider categories, then explain only Cloudflare's US transfers. David now confirms Active24 forwards to Proton. P13 identifies a Swiss processor and Swiss/EEA mail infrastructure; neither notice contains Swiss transfer information. The build checks confirm that omission in both languages.
- Rule and applicability: P01 Art 13(1)(f), applicable since 25 May 2018, requires information about intended third-country transfers and the existence/absence of adequacy or relevant safeguards. P14 currently lists Switzerland as adequate. Adequacy permits the destination; it does not remove transparency duties. Naming recipient categories is permitted by Art 13(1)(e), so requiring every provider's legal name is not the basis of this finding.
- Impact: people writing to the published address cannot learn the actual non-EEA email destination/basis from this notice. This is an incomplete disclosure, not evidence that mail forwarding is unlawful or that consent is required.
- Remediation: update both languages to reflect Active24 forwarding to a Proton inbox and Switzerland's EU adequacy basis; link the relevant provider/Commission information. Keep the private inbox address private. Reconcile any additional account-specific destinations first. In the same notice review, decide a real retention criterion for portfolio-subject data and check Art 14 delivery/source requirements; those off-repository facts are currently unverified, not separately confirmed violations.
- Verification: review EN/CS rendered notices against the real provider map and P13–14; confirm Swiss destination/basis, existing Cloudflare mechanism, consistent recipient categories and working contact links. Record the update date. No live email is needed for this check.

### LEG-009: The ongoing processing has no completed private record

- Severity: **Low**; confidence: **High**; status: **Confirmed**; applicability: **Applies**; verification: **Gap**.
- Previous requirement: LEG-009's processing-record portion, 4 November. Its separate code/content-license recommendation is fulfilled.
- Location: private controller processing record, outside the public repository; no source line applies. `docs/milestone-6-checklist.md`, “Private records,” still has this task open.
- Evidence: David explicitly confirmed none of the requested private records was completed. The site continuously publishes identifiable portfolio subjects and operates ongoing hosting/contact activities. This is positive operator evidence, not an inference from a missing repository file.
- Rule and exception: P01 Art 30(1) defines controller-record contents. Art 30(5)'s under-250 exception does not cover processing that is not occasional; continuous publication is such processing. P02's proposed simplification is still pending on the review date. The earlier report's “recommendation/undetermined” classification is therefore replaced for these established facts.
- Impact: limited accountability and difficulty explaining purposes, recipients, transfers, retention and safeguards if a subject or authority asks. No large-scale or sensitive-data harm was observed.
- Remediation: complete one concise **private** record covering website delivery/security, correspondence, and identifiable people/client work in the portfolio. Include controller/contact, purposes, subject/data categories, recipients, third countries and applicable safeguards, erasure time limits where possible, and a general security description (Art 30(1)(a)–(g)). Adding lawful bases, interests/objections reasoning and the review date is a useful accountability aid. Do not invent Cloudflare retention or backdate completion.
- Verification: David reviews and dates the record; compare each entry with notices, configuration and provider terms. Capture only completion/date in project tracking, not the private record or personal correspondence. Review after any supplier, purpose or feature change.
