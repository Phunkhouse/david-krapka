## Executive summary

The site is a static, cookie-free personal portfolio with no forms, payments or third-party requests. Most consumer, marketing and accessibility regimes do not apply. Four things do matter before launch:

1. **Planned content offers client work, which your current situation rules out (LEG-001).** The design invites potential clients ("Have a project? → Start a project") and shows freelancing as ongoing ("[20XX – now]"). You stated that your trade licence is suspended and the site will not offer paid work for now. Offering services publicly during a suspension is a legal-classification question. The smallest fix is to word the first release as a showcase and job search, and to add the offer only after the IČO is reactivated.
2. **GDPR applies, so a short privacy notice is needed (LEG-003).** A public website is not a "purely personal" activity. Visitors' IP addresses in Cloudflare's request handling, emails people send you, and names of other people in case studies are personal data. A short bilingual privacy notice, linked from the footer, covers this. It needs the public email address, which is still a placeholder.
3. **Permissions for other people and their work (LEG-006).** Showing a person's likeness, for example in a screenshot of Vladan Vaněk's personal website, requires that person's consent under Czech law. Screenshots can also contain other people's copyrighted work. A simple written OK per project covers this.
4. **Business identification later (LEG-002).** Once the site offers your services as an entrepreneur, the Civil Code requires your name, registered address (*sídlo*) and IČO on the site. That forces a decision on whether to publish your home address or use a separate business address.

There is nothing to fix in the current placeholder. Fulfilment of the planned controls is `Not yet assessed`; the Pre-launch audit in milestone 6 checks them.

## Findings

Only LEG-001 is a supported design risk. The other rows above are planned requirements, not findings.

### LEG-001: Planned content offers paid client work while the trade is suspended

- Severity: Medium
- Confidence: Medium (the conflict between plan and stated facts is certain; its legal classification is not)
- Status: Confirmed (design conflict); legal consequence `Needs interpretation`
- Previous finding: none
- Location: `docs/SPEC.md:14` (audience "Potential clients", "Have a project?"); `docs/design/landing-page.md:92` ("Independent · for clients · [20XX – now]"), `docs/design/landing-page.md:120-122` ("Have a project?", "Start a project →"); `docs/decisions/0007-cloudflare-workers-hosting.md:9` ("a portfolio that invites client work")
- Evidence: The operator stated that the trade licence is suspended and the site will not offer paid work for now. During a suspension, the Trade Licensing Act's duties continue (S-06 § 31(11)). A trade is systematic, independent, profit-oriented activity (S-06 § 2). Inviting projects is the public face of that activity, and it would also make the site "information made available to the public" by an entrepreneur under S-05 § 435(1). Counter-evidence considered: the site has no prices, contracts or payments, and showing past freelance work is not by itself operating a trade. That is why this is an interpretation question rather than a confirmed violation.
- Impact: If launched as designed, the site could be read as offering services during the suspension. That could conflict with the suspension or with the conditions of the social-security support, and the Civil Code identification duties would apply without the details being on the site. Separately, the copy would contradict your own stated intent.
- Remediation: Word the first release as a showcase and job search (see LEG-001 in the table): remove or neutralise "Start a project", show freelance as past or project-based without "– now", and update the SPEC audience. Plan the client offer and the LEG-002 identification details for when the IČO is reactivated.
- Verification: Review the built EN and CS landing pages before launch, and in the Pre-launch audit, for offer wording. If you want certainty about what counts as offering services during a suspension, or about your support conditions, ask the trade licensing office or the office paying the support with this question: "May I publish a portfolio of past freelance work, with a contact email, while my trade is suspended, without offering new paid work?"
